The most exposed nation in the hemisphere. Haiti was already in humanitarian crisis before Day 1 — active gang control of Port-au-Prince, collapsed institutions, 77% electricity grid losses (highest in the region), and zero strategic reserves. Africa's largest oil producer and exporter — nominally the biggest beneficiary in Sub-Saharan Africa from a sustained oil price surge. The world's third-largest oil importer absorbs this shock at a systemic scale that no other Global South economy matches. A $10 rise in Brent adds approximately $15–18 billion to India's annual import bill. At $92 Brent that is a $210B+ annualised fuel bill — a significant current account pressure. India has been buying discounted Russian crude since 2022, partially buffering its exposure. But Hormuz carries 90% of the oil flowing to Asian markets — including India's non-Russian supply. The fertilizer compound is also severe: India is a major urea importer.

Sri Lanka's 2022 economic collapse was triggered primarily by a foreign exchange crisis that made fuel unaffordable — queues at petrol stations, blackouts, political revolution. The country emerged from that crisis through IMF intervention and is still in fragile recovery. The Indian Ocean is now an active naval theatre adjacent to Sri Lanka's coastline.

One of the most specific and underreported exposures on this map. The Philippines has over 2 million Overseas Filipino Workers (OFWs) in Saudi Arabia, UAE, Qatar, and Kuwait — the exact countries now under active Iranian missile attack. If the Gulf war escalates and those workers evacuate — or if Gulf economies contract and reduce employment — the Philippine economy faces a dual shock: rising oil import costs AND collapsing remittance inflows simultaneously.

Position
Indonesia left OPEC in 2004 when it became a net oil importer. But it is the world's largest thermal coal exporter and a major nickel producer — and high oil prices typically drive coal and industrial metal demand. The partial hedge means Indonesia is moderately exposed rather than critically. Its large domestic market (~280M people) and commodity diversification give it more resilience than most other Global South importers. Fuel subsidies — already expensive — will be further strained at $90 oil.

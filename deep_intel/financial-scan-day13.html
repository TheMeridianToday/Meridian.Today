Sector Scan · What Each Industry Is Absorbing
⛽
Energy Producers
Windfall

The only clear beneficiary sector in the Global South. Brent at $101+ creates windfall margins for any producer outside the Hormuz-affected zone. Sasol up 40%, Thungela up 57%, Petrobras positioned as top buy by Morningstar and GMO. AngloGold and Gold Fields benefit from gold safe-haven bid as investors flee equities.

The windfall is geographically concentrated: South Africa and Brazil. Gulf producers (QatarEnergy, Aramco, ADNOC, KPC, BAPCO) are in the strike zone and facing force majeure, infrastructure attacks, and rerouting costs that eat into the very price windfall they would otherwise receive.

🖥️
Semiconductors
Exposed

South Korea's chip industry is the war's most important industrial casualty in the Global South. Samsung −9.99%, SK Hynix −11.47% in a single session. KOSPI triggered circuit breakers twice in four days — its worst four-session stretch in years.

Two separate risks: (1) energy cost escalation — semiconductor fabs consume enormous amounts of electricity, now priced at elevated levels; (2) helium supply disruption — Korea sources a portion of production-grade helium from the Middle East. Korean lawmakers confirmed both risks formally to Reuters. Goldman Sachs maintained bullish long-term targets — market bought the dip.

🛩️
Aviation + Shipping
Crisis

Philippine shipping lines confirmed rate hikes of up to 25% as fuel prices soared (BusinessWorld, Mar 10). Airlines across the Global South remain in route review — not cancellations yet, but any $120+ sustained Brent triggers formal restructuring.

VLCC tanker day rates hit $423K/day — all-time record. 16 confirmed shipping attacks in the Persian Gulf since Feb 28. The Thai cargo vessel Mayuree Naree burned in Hormuz. Oman's navy rescued its 20 crew. Three more tankers attacked overnight on Day 12–13. IMO emergency session called for March 18–19 London — first of its kind for a single shipping route in IMO history.

🏦
Banking / Finance
Under Pressure

South African Reserve Bank Governor Lesetja Kganyago confirmed SARB is redrawing risk scenarios ahead of the March 26 rate decision. The previous adverse scenario is "no longer relevant." Rate cuts — which were the consensus expectation at the start of 2026 — are off the table. Rate hikes are now possible.

FirstRand posted strong H1 results but investors shifted defensive. Chatham House identified Egypt and Tunisia as most at risk of bond market destabilisation from oil-price-driven fiscal pressure. Philippines' BSP is now under pressure to raise rates despite a slowing economy — the classic stagflation trap. Lowy Institute flags Bangladesh as exposed to capital flight.

🌾
Agriculture / Fertilizer
September Risk

The slow-moving catastrophe. One-third of globally traded urea moves through the Persian Gulf. QatarEnergy's force majeure halted gas production at Ras Laffan — the same facility that feeds Qatar's fertilizer export infrastructure. European TTF gas prices nearly doubled on the news. LNG spot prices in Asia doubled to $25.40/MMBtu by Day 4.

Northern Hemisphere planting season is now. The fertilizer that should be in the ground in April, May, and June is not being ordered, not being produced, or not being shipped. The food price spike from this disruption will land in September–October 2026 — regardless of when the war ends. The Caribbean, West Africa, and South Asia are the regions most exposed to the downstream food price cascade.

⚡
Power / Utilities
Pass-Through Active

Meralco confirmed rate increases in March's billing cycle — fuel cost pass-through is immediate in the Philippines under its regulatory model. Jamaica's JPS electricity surcharge mechanism activated automatically when Brent crossed $80. Caribbean utilities operate on similar automatic adjustment formulas.

India's government is prioritising household gas supply over commercial users — restaurants warning of shutdown. Pakistan's grid runs on Gulf LNG: Qatar force majeure directly hits generation capacity. Bangladesh grid same exposure. Sri Lanka: 2022 structural conditions reassembling at speed. South Africa: Sasol supplies 30% of liquid fuel — any disruption to its inputs is a national energy security event.

Meridian · Financial Scan · Day 13 · March 13, 2026
Two wars.
One for the Global South.
One against it.

The Global South is not a single economic actor in this war. It is two groups moving in opposite directions simultaneously. South Africa's energy producers — Sasol up 40%, Thungela up 57%, gold miners elevated — are capturing a windfall from the same Brent price that is destroying purchasing power for every oil-importing economy in the region. The JSE lost R2 trillion in one week. Sasol gained R10 billion in one day. Both are South Africa.

South Korea is the most important industrial casualty of this war that nobody is discussing loudly enough. Samsung −9.99%. SK Hynix −11.47%. Circuit breakers twice in four days on the KOSPI. These are not portfolio losses — they represent the world's most sophisticated semiconductor supply chains absorbing energy price shocks and helium supply disruption simultaneously. The downstream effect on global chip pricing will be felt in every device manufactured in the next 12 months.

The Philippines, with 50–60 days of oil reserves and 98% Middle East crude dependence, is the most exposed mid-size economy to a prolonged war. The peso at ₱59.50 record low, PSEi at its worst level since December 2025, Meralco rates up, BSP rate cut cancelled. Every transmission mechanism for oil price pain is active simultaneously. There is no buffer left to deploy.

QatarEnergy's force majeure is the quiet catastrophe. Ras Laffan supplies 20% of global LNG. That 20% feeds Asia's power grids — Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Japan, South Korea. The IEA's reserve release covers oil. There is no comparable strategic reserve for LNG. The war solved half the energy equation and left the other half unaddressed.

Petrobras is the one Global South giant positioned to gain cleanly — offshore Brazil, unaffected supply chain, 6.3% free cash flow yield at current Brent prices. That is a fact worth holding alongside all of the above: not every Global South economy is losing this war in equal measure. Those with domestic production capacity and no Hormuz exposure are positioned as beneficiaries. The rest are absorbing a shock that compounds daily.

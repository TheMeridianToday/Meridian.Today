March 26, 2026 · The Extension
Ten more days.
Iran rejects.
The South pays.
President Trump granted a 10-day extension on strikes against Iranian power plants, moving the deadline to April 6 at 8:00 PM ET. Iran responded by rejecting the US proposal as "one-sided and unfair," issuing five counter-conditions including war reparations and sovereignty over the Strait of Hormuz. Pakistan confirmed it is facilitating "indirect talks" and that the 15 points are "being deliberated." Israel struck Isfahan regardless, killing 12. While diplomats negotiate, South Africa prepares for diesel at R30/litre -- the first time ever. Jamaica implemented its fifth fuel price increase in March. It is not a reprieve for the Global South.
The Ten-Day Extension · Diplomatic Limbo
US-Iran · March 26 · Deadline Shifted
April 6, 8:00 PM.
The new deadline.
If talks fail, strikes resume.

President Trump announced Thursday that he had extended the deadline for strikes against Iranian power plants by 10 days, citing a request from the Iranian government and "productive talks." The new deadline is Monday, April 6, 2026, at 8:00 PM Eastern Time [^128^].

"As per Iranian Government request, please let this statement serve to represent that I am pausing the period of Energy Plant destruction by 10 Days," Trump wrote on Truth Social. "Talks are ongoing and, despite erroneous statements to the contrary by the Fake News Media, and others, they are going very well" [^128^].

The extension follows Iran's formal response to the 15-point US proposal delivered via Pakistan. Iranian officials rejected the proposal as "one-sided and unfair," stating it "did not meet minimum expectations." Iran issued five counter-conditions: end to aggression, concrete guarantees against future war, war reparations and compensation, comprehensive ceasefire across all fronts including allied groups, and recognition of Iranian sovereignty over the Strait of Hormuz [^120^][^121^][^126^][^130^].

Pakistan's Foreign Minister confirmed his country is facilitating "indirect talks" and that the 15 points are "being deliberated upon by Iran." US envoy Steve Witkoff confirmed presenting the proposal through Pakistan as mediator [^128^].

The Five Counter-Conditions
Iran's response transforms the diplomatic landscape from a US-imposed framework to a bilateral negotiation. The demands for reparations and Hormuz sovereignty are non-starters for Washington -- the former requiring acknowledgment of damages the US denies causing, the latter ceding control of an international waterway. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi stated explicitly: "We do not want a ceasefire. We want the war to end in a way that it does not repeat, on our own terms." The ten-day extension is not a pause in hostilities but a pause in escalation -- Israel continues strikes, Iran continues missile attacks, and the Global South continues absorbing economic shock. Israel · Operations Regardless
Military Track · March 26 · Parallel War
Isfahan bombed.
12 killed.
"Unchanged plan."

Israel launched a wave of strikes on Tehran and central Iran on Thursday, targeting what it described as Iran's "most central explosives production facility" in Isfahan. The strikes killed 12 and wounded 28 [^128^].

The military track operates independently of diplomacy. Israeli Defense Forces spokesperson stated operations would continue "according to an unchanged plan, regardless of any diplomatic efforts" [^128^]. Iran responded with two rounds of missile attacks on central Israel, causing destruction and injuries, and rocket fire from Lebanon killed an Israeli soldier [^128^].

Kuwait International Airport's fuel tank was hit by a drone and burst into flames -- the latest expansion of Iranian attacks to all six GCC states [^126^]. The two-track war is now explicit: diplomacy in Pakistan, military action in the Gulf and Levant.

South Africa · The R30 Threshold
Southern Africa · March 26 · Confirmed
Diesel: R30.
Petrol: R26.
The worst ever.

The Central Energy Fund's March 26 snapshot confirms the worst-case scenario. Diesel will increase by R9.86 for 500ppm and R10.00 for 50ppm, pushing retail prices above R30 per litre for the first time in South African history. Petrol increases by R5.24 (93 octane) and R5.76 (95 octane), bringing 95 unleaded to R26.27 inland -- approaching the July 2022 record of R26.74 [^119^][^125^][^127^].

The wholesale diesel price is currently R17.84 at the coast; retail margins add R2-3. The final retail price will exceed R30. The under-recovery has reached staggering levels due to Brent averaging $95 in March versus $69 in February -- a 38% surge [^119^].

The April 1 tax increases add insult: 21 cents per litre additional fuel taxes (General Fuel Levy +9c, Carbon Levy +5c, Road Accident Fund +7c) [^119^]. The DA has proposed cutting fuel levies by 50% to reduce prices by R3.17/litre -- a R6.5 billion monthly revenue sacrifice [^127^].

The Reserve Bank offers no relief. Governor Lesetja Kganyago has confirmed rate cuts are "firmly off the table." Investec projects the fuel shock alone will add 1.0% to CPI, pushing inflation toward 4.0% in Q2 2026 [^119^].

Jamaica · Five Hikes in March
Caribbean · March 26 · No Relief
90-octane: $175.32.
Diesel: $180.25.
ULSD: $187.09.

Petrojam Limited implemented its fifth fuel price increase of March on Thursday, March 26. Every product rose by $4.50 per litre: 87-octane gasoline to $167.88, 90-octane to $175.32, automotive diesel to $180.25, ultra-low sulphur diesel to $187.09, and kerosene to $169.64 [^115^][^110^].

The cumulative March increase is stark. From March 5 to March 26: 90-octane rose from $161.83 to $175.32 -- a $13.49 increase in 21 days. Diesel climbed from $166.75 to $180.25. ULSD -- the standard for modern engines -- now approaches $190 [^110^].

Marketing companies and retailers add their own markups to these ex-refinery prices. The pump price paid by Jamaican motorists and businesses exceeds $180 for regular gasoline and $190 for clean diesel.

The IEA's global policy tracker reveals Jamaica has no emergency energy conservation measures in place. While Bangladesh limits AC to 25°C, Pakistan implements 4-day work weeks, and Sri Lanka deploys QR-code fuel rationing, Jamaica's response is limited to Petrojam's weekly price adjustments [^124^].

Nigeria · The Gantry-Retail Divide
West Africa · March 26 · Partial Relief
Gantry: N1,200.
Retail: N1,350.
The cut does not reach pumps.

Dangote Petroleum Refinery reduced its gantry price for petrol to N1,200 per litre on March 26, down from N1,275 -- a N75 decrease [^122^][^123^]. The move followed five price hikes in March that had pushed the refinery's gantry price from N774 to N1,275, a cumulative 64.7% increase [^95^].

But the reduction has not transmitted to retail. Independent marketers and stations continue selling at N1,250-1,350 per litre. Roads in Jigawa State were "unusually quiet" as prices surged to nearly N1,500 per litre at some stations, forcing commuters to stay home [^118^].

The stickiness has structural causes. The refinery's coastal price -- what marketers pay for delivery -- remains elevated at N1,646,748 per metric tonne. The naira trades at N1,650 to the dollar. And with global Brent at $100, import parity pricing keeps domestic prices anchored to international markets regardless of local refining capacity [^99^].

The Dangote refinery has become a magnet for African buyers seeking alternatives to Hormuz-dependent supply. South Africa, Ghana, and Kenya have formally reached out; several others are making enquiries. But the 650,000-barrel-per-day facility cannot satisfy regional demand at prices disconnected from global markets [^95^].

Caribbean · 24 Days of Silence
Regional Response · March 2 vs. March 26
Barbados hedges.
Jamaica hikes.
No coordination.

Twenty-four days have passed since CARICOM's last coordinated statements on March 2. The regional fragmentation is now starkly visible in the IEA policy tracker: Barbados appears with comprehensive hedging and VAT caps; Jamaica appears with price adjustments only [^124^].

The contrast in outcomes is measurable. Barbados has locked in heavy fuel oil at $92/barrel for three months and capped VAT on diesel until March 2027. Jamaica's 90-octane gasoline has risen from $161.83 to $175.32 in 21 days -- an 8.3% increase with no ceiling [^110^].

Trinidad & Tobago and Guyana -- the region's oil producers -- remain absent from the policy tracker entirely. No deployment of windfall revenues. No regional energy security framework. No coordinated strategic reserve. The $130 billion Vaca Muerta investment in Argentina highlights what coordinated resource development looks like; the Caribbean's 15 million people face 40%+ electricity surcharges without equivalent coordination [^135^].

The ten-day extension does not extend to CARICOM's silence. While US and Iranian diplomats negotiate in Pakistan, the Caribbean has no seat at any table -- diplomatic, energy, or financial. The 24-day silence is not neutrality. It is an active policy of absorption without response.

War · Day 6 · Morning Update

Iran Is Now Hitting Every Gulf Capital Simultaneously

The death toll inside Iran has crossed 1,230 killed and over 6,000 wounded since Saturday. Israel announced a new "wave of strikes" against military infrastructure in Tehran overnight. Explosions were reported across Iran — in Tehran, in the holy city of Qom, in Sanandaj. US and Western officials stated that they have "effectively neutralised" a significant portion of Iran's military capabilities in six days. Iran's navy, air force, air detection, and radar systems have been described by Trump as largely destroyed.

"America is winning. We are accelerating, not decelerating. The operation is only four days old — metrics are shifting, dust is settling and more forces are arriving."

— Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, Pentagon briefing, March 4, 2026

But Iran's ability to strike back has not been neutralised. Today Iran fired 14 ballistic missiles and 4 drones at Doha, Qatar — the capital of the nation that hosts the US Fifth Fleet's air operations hub at Al Udeid Air Base. Qatar's air defences intercepted 13 of the 14 missiles. One fell in Qatari territorial waters. Qatar's interior ministry ordered evacuations near the US Embassy in Doha. Authorities sent an "elevated" emergency alert to all Doha residents — the kind sent when attacks are imminent or active.

Bahrain's state-run oil refinery was struck by an Iranian missile. Fire extinguished, facility still operational — but the strategic message is clear: Iran is now targeting Gulf energy infrastructure directly. An oil tanker flying the Bahamas flag was struck by a boat at the Iraqi port of Khor al-Zubair, causing damage and an oil spill. Oman reported one of its oil storage tanks damaged in an "incident." This is now a coordinated campaign against the region's energy export infrastructure, not just military assets.

The Lebanon front: Israel expanded its evacuation orders to cover the entirety of Beirut's southern suburbs — home to hundreds of thousands of people. Israel then struck Tripoli in northern Lebanon for the first time in this conflict, killing a Hamas commander. Far-right Finance Minister Smotrich warned southern Beirut "will be turned to rubble." A Kurdish ground offensive in northwest Iran is also underway, with US officials reportedly asking Iraqi Kurds to assist in cross-border operations.

Geopolitics · The Europe Shift

NATO Fires On Iran. Europe Can No Longer Stay Out.

The single most significant geopolitical development of Day 6 is not another strike on Tehran. It is this: NATO air defence systems intercepted an Iranian ballistic missile traveling toward Turkey's airspace — passing through Iraqi and Syrian airspace before it reached a NATO member state. That is the first time in history NATO forces have engaged an Iranian munition targeting a member country. Iran denied firing at Turkey. NATO says it happened. The alliance is now, functionally, a participant.

Europe's other moves today: France authorised the US to use French military bases and sent its aircraft carrier toward the Mediterranean. The UK has permitted the US to use British bases for defensive operations. Canada's Prime Minister Mark Carney said he "cannot categorically rule out participation." Germany's Merz said there remain "many questions" but declared firm support for the operation's goals.

Against this: Spain explicitly denied the use of its military bases for US flights connected to an offensive against Iran. Trump has threatened economic retaliation. Ukraine — already fighting its own war — offered to deploy drone-defence experts to the Gulf to help defend against Iranian drone swarms. The world is drawing battle lines. Every country is being forced to pick a posture.

Iran's Foreign Ministry warned today that EU countries "will pay the price, sooner or later" if they remain silent over the US-Israeli attacks. That message, paired with the NATO intercept, makes European diplomatic neutrality mathematically impossible to sustain.

Morning Verdict · March 5, 2026 · Day 6
THE WAR JUST GOT BIGGER. AGAIN.

Six days. The war has now crossed every containment boundary that was drawn. Iran is hitting Doha with ballistic missiles. A refinery in Bahrain is on fire. NATO has intercepted an Iranian munition over a member state for the first time in history. France's aircraft carrier is moving. Canada won't rule out joining. Ukraine is offering its drone experts to Gulf states. This is no longer a Middle East story. This is a global story with a Middle East epicentre.

The succession crisis is the most dangerous variable nobody is talking about. Iran's Assembly of Experts was bombed mid-session on Day 4. Mojtaba Khamenei — the late leader's son — is emerging as the front-runner. Trump is already saying his selection would be "unacceptable" and insisting the US must have a role in choosing Iran's next leader. That is an extraordinary claim that has no historical precedent in international relations. It tells you the US sees this not as a military operation with an end, but as a regime-replacement project. Regime replacement in a country with Iran's geography, its proxies, its nuclear-adjacent infrastructure, and its IRGC units spread across four countries is not a 4-week operation. It is a generational one.

For the Global South — for Jamaica, for the Caribbean, for every small economy watching from outside the blast radius — the pattern today is the one that matters most. Iran is targeting economic infrastructure, not just military targets. A refinery in Bahrain. A tanker in Iraqi waters. Storage tanks in Oman. This is a deliberate campaign to make energy exports from the Gulf unreliable, expensive, and dangerous. Every barrel of Jamaican import-dependent fuel, every containerised food shipment, every airline fuel uplift at Kingston — all of it is priced against a market that is now being systematically attacked. The war didn't come to the Caribbean. But its bill did.

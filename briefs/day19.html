The F-35 · A Line That Has Never Been Crossed
US Military · March 19 · Combat First
Iran shot an F-35 today.
It has never been done
before in the aircraft's history.

Iranian forces damaged a US Air Force F-35 on Thursday, the first time the stealth fighter has been hit in active combat since entering service. The aircraft was damaged rather than destroyed. Details on the crew were not immediately released. The incident confirms that Iran's air defence systems retain the capability to reach aircraft operating at the altitudes and speeds the F-35 uses for its strike missions.

The F-35 has been the primary platform for deep-penetration strikes on Iranian nuclear and military infrastructure throughout this conflict. Its stealth design was considered a core operational advantage over Iranian air defences. A successful hit, regardless of the method, changes the risk calculation for every mission profile currently being flown over Iranian territory. The US military did not issue a formal statement by publication time.

The Pentagon has separately requested $200 billion in new war funding from Congress. Trump described the request as "a small price to pay." Three days before the F-35 incident, six US airmen were confirmed dead following the KC-135 crash over Iraq on March 12.

Trump, Israel, and the Energy Strike Split
Washington · Tel Aviv · Qatar · March 19
Trump told Israel to stop.
CNN says the US
approved the strike anyway.

President Trump told Israel on Thursday to stop targeting Iranian energy infrastructure, according to CNN. The instruction followed the South Pars strike on Wednesday and the market reaction that pushed Brent to $110 overnight. CNN separately reported that US intelligence sources confirmed Washington was aware of Israel's plan to strike South Pars before it happened, despite Trump's public statements to the contrary.

Iran struck Qatar on Thursday in direct retaliation. Fires were reported near Doha after Iranian strikes hit Qatari energy installations. Qatar had condemned the South Pars attack on Wednesday as dangerous and irresponsible. Iran's foreign minister confirmed that Tehran has entered what he described as a regional war and that the Strait of Hormuz will not return to pre-war conditions regardless of how the conflict ends.

A senior Iranian security official told CNN that Trump was aware of the full details of the energy infrastructure attacks and the assassinations of Iranian officials throughout the conflict. The official also stated Iran is confident the US approved or was informed of all major Israeli operations. That framing contradicts the White House's public position on all of it. The gap between what Washington says publicly and what Tehran says Washington approved has widened with each passing week.

Airlines · $175 Jet Fuel and What It Costs You
Aviation · Global · March 19
Jet fuel doubled in 19 days.
Airfares are following.
Caribbean routes already up.

Jet fuel was priced between $85 and $90 a barrel before February 28. The IATA fuel monitor recorded it at $175 a barrel on Thursday, following an 11.2 percent increase in the past week alone. For airlines, fuel represents the single largest component of operating costs on most routes, and a move of this scale requires an immediate response.

Air New Zealand announced the cancellation of 1,100 flights affecting over 44,000 passengers and suspended its 2026 financial guidance entirely. Chief Executive Nikhil Ravishankar described the speed of the fuel cost increase as unprecedented. Thai Airways told investors it plans to raise ticket prices by 10 to 15 percent and advised customers to book now. SAS, Qantas, and Air France-KLM have all added fuel surcharges. Deutsche Bank calculated that transcontinental airfares have increased more than 100 percent on some routes, with Caribbean, Florida, and transatlantic destinations among those rising.

IATA Director General Willie Walsh told reporters that fare increases are not optional at current fuel prices. US domestic fares have not yet moved sharply, but Deutsche Bank estimated a minimum 11 percent increase is required to offset fuel costs at $115 Brent. A sustained $115 environment makes that a floor, not a ceiling.

Caribbean Routes · The Specific Exposure
Deutsche Bank's analysis named Caribbean routes among those where fares have already risen. This operates on two levels simultaneously. Residents travelling regionally or to North America are paying higher fares. Tourists travelling to the Caribbean face higher costs, which reduces inbound visitor numbers at the same moment hotel and food costs are rising due to the same oil shock. Tourism-dependent Caribbean economies face a demand-side reduction on top of a cost-side increase. Neither mechanism comes with a government-supplied buffer. Source: Deutsche Bank · IATA · CBS News
Diplomacy · Iran's Terms, Japan's Ask, the ECB's Warning
Ceasefire Track · Tokyo · Tehran · Frankfurt · March 19
Iran says it never
asked to stop. Trump says
the war ends very soon.

Japan's Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi met Trump at the White House on Thursday. The primary agenda item was Hormuz security. Japan imports approximately 90 percent of its oil from the Middle East and is among the countries Trump has said should send warships to help secure the strait. The ECB held interest rates unchanged on Thursday, citing "massive uncertainty" from the energy shock, noting that the war's effect on inflation makes the 2026 rate path impossible to project.

Iran's Foreign Minister Araghchi has confirmed that multiple countries have approached Tehran seeking arrangements for their vessels to transit Hormuz. He said some are already passing under what Iran describes as a selective permission system. That system is not a ceasefire. It is leverage. Bloomberg's assessment from earlier in the week stands: full Hormuz restoration requires a ceasefire. There is no ceasefire framework in place.

Trump has said repeatedly the war will end soon and that Iran wants to negotiate. Iran's foreign ministry has stated the opposite position at every press conference this week. The IMO emergency session concluded in London on Wednesday with no binding resolution on Hormuz passage. The gap between the stated end positions of the two sides has not narrowed in 19 days.

The F-35 was built on the premise of operational invulnerability over contested airspace. Iran tested that premise on Thursday. Whether through a new method, an exploited vulnerability, or a specific altitude engagement, the result is the same: the aircraft that has been conducting the deepest strikes inside Iran since Day 1 is now confirmed hittable. The US military has not said how it happened. The oil market priced the news and kept moving upward to $115.

Trump is telling Israel to stop targeting Iranian energy infrastructure while CNN's sources confirm the United States approved the South Pars strike that caused Wednesday's escalation. Iran responded by striking Qatar, which supplies a fifth of global LNG and directly feeds the power grids of Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Japan and South Korea. The chain of energy infrastructure attacks that began as a threat on Day 14 is now a two-sided campaign with no agreed boundary and no ceasefire framework.

For the economies Meridian covers, Thursday's number is jet fuel at $175 a barrel and transcontinental airfares up over 100 percent. Inbound tourism to the Caribbean faces a demand reduction. Outbound travel from South Asian diaspora communities is more expensive. Freight and logistics costs for import-dependent economies rise with every cargo carrier that adds a surcharge. The $115 oil price is the visible signal. The airfare increase, the remittance compression, and the food price spike arriving in September are the structural consequences that will outlast the war itself by months.

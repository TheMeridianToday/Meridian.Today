Food Security · Emerging Crisis

The Strait Is Blocking More Than Oil. It Is Blocking Food For The Global South.

A development that has been largely missing from mainstream coverage but is critical for Caribbean and Global South audiences: the Hormuz closure is not only an energy crisis. It is a food supply crisis arriving at the worst possible moment. The Strait is a key corridor for transporting fertilizer ingredients — ammonia, urea, and potash — to the world. Iran is the world's third-largest urea exporter. The Strait carries 25–35% of globally traded fertilizer.

And this blockage is arriving just as the Northern Hemisphere enters planting season. Farms that cannot source fertilizer at planting do not produce at harvest. Traders are already pricing this: fertilizer companies CF Industries (+5.08%), Bunge (+3.14%), and Archer Daniels Midland (+1.37%) were among the only stocks in the green on Wall Street today as markets began to grasp the food supply chain consequences of a sustained Hormuz closure. Packaged food companies also advanced.

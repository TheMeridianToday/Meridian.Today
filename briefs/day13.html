March 13, 2026 · The War Widens
US aircraft down in Iraq.
French soldier dead.
Russia oil sanctions lifted.
A US Air Force KC-135 refuelling aircraft crashed in Iraq on Thursday — the fifth US aircraft lost since the war began. A French soldier was killed in Erbil. Trump lifted restrictions on Russian oil sales in an emergency measure to relieve the price shock. The IEA declared this the largest oil supply disruption in the history of the global oil market — 8 million barrels per day offline this month alone. The markets absorbed it all and Brent stayed above $100.
Military · The War's Expanding Perimeter
US Military · France · March 13
Fifth US aircraft lost.
A French soldier buried.
NATO's edge is showing.

A US Air Force Boeing KC-135 Stratotanker — a refuelling aircraft — came down in Iraq on Thursday. The US military confirmed the loss, stating the aircraft was not brought down by hostile or friendly fire. It is the fifth US aircraft lost since Operation Epic Fury began on February 28. The crew's status was not immediately confirmed.

French President Emmanuel Macron confirmed the first French military fatality of the war: Chief Warrant Officer Arnaud Frion, killed in an attack in Iraq's Erbil region. Several other French soldiers were wounded in the same strike. Macron called the attack "unacceptable" and said France's presence in Iraq was part of anti-terrorism operations. "The war in Iran cannot justify such attacks," Macron said.

France is now a bereaved nation in a war its parliament has not voted to authorise. Macron has been one of the three non-US powers — alongside China and Russia — in ceasefire contact with Tehran. His country now has a soldier's body to bring home. How that shapes France's diplomatic posture over the coming days is the question every chancellery is watching.

Where The War Stands · Aircraft Count
Five US aircraft lost in 13 days. A French soldier killed. Twenty-seven fighters from Iraq's Popular Mobilization Forces killed in US strikes targeting PMF headquarters across multiple Iraqi provinces. The war that was described by Trump as hitting only Iranian military targets has now produced casualties in Iraq, France, Lebanon, the Gulf states, Israel, and Iran. The geographic perimeter of active harm is now wider than the stated scope of the operation.
Energy · The Largest Disruption In History
Energy Markets · IEA · US Treasury · March 13
Trump lifted Russia
oil sanctions. Brent
stayed above $100.

The US Treasury issued an emergency licence Thursday permitting countries to temporarily purchase Russian oil products currently stranded at sea. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent described it as a "narrowly tailored, short-term measure" applying only to oil already in transit. The Kremlin confirmed simultaneously that Moscow and Washington are in discussions about cooperating to stabilise energy markets disrupted by the Hormuz closure.

The measure did not move markets meaningfully. Brent crude settled above $100 per barrel for the second consecutive day — the first time that has happened since August 2022. The IEA published its monthly oil report Thursday, calling the war-driven disruption "the largest supply disruption in the history of the global oil market." The agency said crude and oil product flows through Hormuz had slowed to "a trickle," with supplies set to drop by 8 million barrels per day this month.

The 400 million barrel coordinated IEA reserve release, announced Wednesday, has provided a floor but not a ceiling. Iran's new Supreme Leader vowed in his first statement that Hormuz will remain closed as a "tool of pressure." The IRGC separately warned it will set the region's oil and gas "on fire" if Iranian energy infrastructure and ports are targeted. Three more tankers were attacked overnight, bringing the total shipping incidents in the Persian Gulf to more than 19 since February 28.

The Russia Sanctions Lift — What It Signals
The US spent three years pressuring allies to limit Russian oil revenue as part of Ukraine sanctions policy. On Day 13 of the Iran war, Trump reversed that position by emergency licence to address a supply shock. The Kremlin called it evidence that "interaction between Russia and the United States on energy markets could and should be an important factor in stabilising those markets." Russia now has a seat at the table on both the energy and diplomatic dimensions of this war. That is a significant strategic shift in under two weeks of conflict.
Leadership · A Leader Nobody Has Seen
Iran Leadership · Day 5 of Mojtaba's Rule
"Alive in some form.
Damaged." Trump's
assessment of Iran's leader.

In a Fox News interview recorded Thursday and set to air Friday, Trump was asked about Iran's new Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei — who has still not appeared on camera or produced audio since his election on Day 9. Trump said: "I think he probably is. I think he is damaged, but I think he's probably alive in some form." The President of the United States was speculating about the medical condition of the man who leads the country the US is at war with.

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu was more direct. At his first press conference since the war began, he said Khamenei "cannot show his face in public" and that he "wouldn't take out any life insurance" on the new Supreme Leader. The statement is a public assassination threat against a sitting head of state.

Khamenei's first statement — read by a state TV anchor over a still photograph, with no audio or video of the man himself — vowed Hormuz would remain closed and US bases would be attacked until evacuated. Iranian media has offered no update on his location or physical condition. Iran is now being led by a man whose whereabouts, health, and decision-making capacity are unknown to every government in the world, including those trying to negotiate a ceasefire.

Thailand became the latest Global South government to formally confront Iran over the cost of this war. Thailand's Foreign Affairs Ministry conveyed its "strongest protest" to the Iranian ambassador in Bangkok after the Thai cargo vessel Mayuree Naree was struck and set ablaze in the Strait of Hormuz. The deputy permanent secretary for foreign affairs requested a written apology and clarification of facts from Iranian authorities. The Royal Thai Navy published photographs of the burning vessel.

Humanitarian + Nuclear · What Isn't Being Said
Civilian Toll · Nuclear Site · School Strike Investigation
A nuclear weapons
test facility struck.
A school probe begins.

Satellite imagery published Thursday confirmed that Israel struck Iran's Taleghan 2 facility at the Parchin military complex — a site assessed by analysts to be capable of testing nuclear weapon components. The facility had been under reconstruction since last May. It is the first confirmed strike on a site directly associated with nuclear weapons development capability, separate from enrichment facilities.

In Washington, 46 Senate Democrats sent a formal letter to Defence Secretary Hegseth demanding an investigation into the Day 1 US strike on an elementary school in Iran. NBC News reported that two people familiar with internal reviews confirmed US forces were likely responsible and that outdated targeting intelligence was most probably used. The majority of those killed were girls between the ages of 7 and 12. No investigation has formally been opened. No individual has been held accountable.

The UN's Tom Fletcher, undersecretary-general for humanitarian affairs, told the Security Council that tens of thousands of Syrians and Lebanese are now crossing from Lebanon into Syria — a country that has spent five years rebuilding from its own civil war and has no capacity to absorb a secondary displacement crisis. Iran's Red Crescent Society counts more than 1,200 Iranian civilians killed. Iran's police chief warned Thursday that those "supporting the country's enemies" will be treated as enemies — a signal of internal repression alongside the external war.

Diplomacy · Iran's World Cup Exit
Ceasefire Track · Football · Cultural Fallout
Iran will not play
in the World Cup.
The exit ramp is still forming.

Iran's Sports Minister Ahmad Donyamali confirmed Thursday that Iran's men's national football team will not participate in the 2026 FIFA World Cup, regardless of Trump's public statements about whether they would be welcome. FIFA has not announced a contingency plan for replacing Iran in the tournament draw. A team has never been removed from a World Cup in the modern era. The tournament, co-hosted by the US, Mexico, and Canada, begins in June.

On the ceasefire track: Iran's President Pezeshkian confirmed he has spoken directly with Russia's Putin and Pakistan's Prime Minister Sharif. Iran's three formal conditions — rights recognition, reparations, and binding future guarantees — remain on the table. The US has not formally responded. The IMO emergency session is scheduled for March 18–19 in London. Energy Secretary Wright said the US may be able to begin escorting vessels through Hormuz by end of month — a statement that, unlike his deleted tweet of Thursday, has not been retracted.

Meridian · Day 13 · March 13, 2026
The war produced
its first NATO casualty.
And its first Russian deal.

Chief Warrant Officer Arnaud Frion is the first soldier from a NATO ally — outside the United States — to be killed in this war. France is now a bereaved nation in a conflict its legislature did not authorise, whose ceasefire contacts with Tehran it has been quietly advancing. His death changes the political calculus for Macron in ways that are not yet visible but will become so.

The Russia sanctions lift is the day's most structurally significant move. The United States spent three years building a sanctions architecture around Russian energy to limit Moscow's revenue and strategic leverage. On Day 13 of the Iran war, that architecture was suspended by emergency licence. Russia immediately framed it as the beginning of energy market cooperation with Washington. The war that started as a US-Israel operation against Iran has now drawn Russia into an energy partnership role with the United States and a diplomatic intermediary role with Tehran. That is a realignment that will outlast the ceasefire, whenever it comes.

For the Global South: the IEA's declaration of the largest supply disruption in history is the number that matters today. Eight million barrels per day offline this month. The reserve release covers part of that. Russian oil covers part of that. The gap — still significant — falls on the countries that are not IEA members, that cannot access Russian supply through waiver, and that have no strategic petroleum reserve of their own. Thailand just lost a cargo ship. The Philippines has 50 days of oil. Pakistan is raising interest rates into a recession. And CARICOM has issued nothing.

Day 13. The war continues at full intensity. The ceasefire architecture is forming. The cascade does not wait for it to finish.

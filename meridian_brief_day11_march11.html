Day 11 · The War Enters Its Second Week

moved oil $20 in thirty minutes.

The price "collapse" today is not relief — Brent is still 25% above where it was two weeks ago. Every surcharge, every disrupted supply chain, every remittance delayed is still active. What happened today was a deleted social media post, an IEA reserve release that excludes the Global South, and Iran and Hezbollah launching their first fully coordinated joint operation of the war — 100+ rockets on northern Israel as Israeli strikes lit Beirut's skyline orange. Markets · The Story That Should Embarrass Everyone
Energy Markets · March 11 · The Most Extraordinary Moment of the War
An official posted
a tweet. Then deleted it.
Oil moved $20.

US Energy Secretary Chris Wright posted — then deleted — a claim that the US Navy had successfully escorted an oil tanker through the Strait of Hormuz. Oil fell more than 17% within thirty minutes. Then the White House clarified: "The US Navy has not escorted a tanker or a vessel at this time." Oil partially recovered. It was the most volatile single-day swing of the war.

What this tells you is not really about the tweet. It tells you how fragile and information-starved this market has become. A market with normal liquidity and normal volume does not move 17% on a social media post. It moves on verified supply data. The fact that it did — and then snapped back when the claim was denied — means the market is running on sentiment, rumour, and fear rather than fundamentals. That is an extremely dangerous environment for any economy dependent on stable energy pricing.

The partial rebound to ~$87.80 still leaves Brent +25% above the pre-war $70 baseline. The IEA's 400 million barrel strategic reserve release — the largest coordinated release in the organisation's history — provided some floor. But analysts were clear: words aren't going to talk oil prices back to normal. The strait is the key. The insurance withdrawal has not been reversed. Hormuz is still at 13 vessels a day versus the normal 153.

The IEA Release — And Who It Doesn't Help
The IEA's 400 million barrel release is the largest coordinated strategic reserve action in the organisation's 50-year history. It will stabilise prices for IEA member economies. IEA members are: Europe, North America, Japan, South Korea, Australia, New Zealand. Pakistan is not a member. The Philippines is not a member. Haiti is not a member. Jamaica is not a member. Kenya is not a member. Bangladesh is not a member. The countries that can least absorb $88/barrel oil are exactly the countries outside the reserve pool. The release helps the alliance that organised the war. It does not help the people absorbing its cost.
Military · The War's New Architecture
Military Operations · March 11 · Escalation Confirmed
Iran and Hezbollah
launched their first
joint operation.

Iran's IRGC described tonight's assault on Israel as a "joint and integrated operation" with Hezbollah — the first time the two have formally coordinated strikes since the war began. Hezbollah launched more than 100 rockets from multiple positions in Lebanon — the largest single Hezbollah barrage since hostilities intensified on March 2. Simultaneously, Iran launched ballistic missiles from the east. Both were largely intercepted. Two people in northern Israel were lightly injured by debris.

Israel's response was immediate and heavy: large-scale strikes on Beirut's southern suburb of Dahiyeh — the second time in days Israel has targeted central Beirut. Israel is now warning that if Lebanon's government does not rein in Hezbollah, it will begin striking Lebanese national civilian infrastructure. A senior IDF official told reporters both sides are "heading toward serious escalation" and that Israel has "more capabilities and tools" it has not yet used.

Iran's military also threatened to strike ports and docks across the Gulf region if Iran's own ports are attacked. Iran called the day's operations its "most intense and heaviest" since the war began — targeting Israel, Persian Gulf nations, and ships in the Strait of Hormuz simultaneously.

"We are heading toward serious escalation. Hezbollah wants to create a new balance in which Israel's enforcement policy in Lebanon ends — and we don't attack at all. That won't happen."
Senior IDF official · March 11, 2026
Mojtaba Khamenei — His Condition, His Silence
CNN reported Wednesday that Mojtaba Khamenei — Iran's new Supreme Leader, elected under bombardment on Day 9 — suffered a fractured foot and minor injuries in the opening US-Israeli strikes on February 28. He has still not spoken publicly since his election. His first statement remains the single most consequential pending event in the oil market: any diplomatic signal moves Brent −$10 immediately; any confrontational statement risks the $120 break. The silence is now Day 3 of his leadership.
Lebanon · The Front That Changed Everything
Humanitarian Crisis · Lebanon · Day 9 of Hezbollah Front
634 dead.
91 children.
800,000 displaced.

Israel's strikes in Lebanon since March 2 have killed 634 people according to Lebanon's Ministry of Public Health — 91 of them children. 800,000 people have been displaced. Less than a quarter of those displaced have found space in government shelters, which a Norwegian Refugee Council official described as "super rough conditions."

On Wednesday, Israeli strikes hit an apartment block in central Beirut — the second time in days the capital's centre has been targeted directly. A Red Cross medic, Youssef Assaf, killed in strikes on southern Lebanon earlier this week, was buried today. A Catholic priest, Pierre al-Rahi, killed in separate strikes, was also buried today. Pope Leo offered condolences. The ICRC mourned Assaf in a statement saying he "lost his life while carrying out his humanitarian duty."

Backlash against Hezbollah is growing inside Lebanon. Lebanese voices are increasingly and openly blaming Hezbollah for triggering a war the country cannot survive. Lebanon's government last week outlawed Hezbollah's military activities — but Israel's ambassador to the UN asked publicly: "Where are the seizures of their weapons? Where is your military?"

Humanitarian · The Numbers Behind The Numbers
Humanitarian Crisis · UN Warning · Day 11
Toxic black rain.
Humanitarian supply chains
cut off.

The United Nations issued a formal warning Wednesday of toxic black rain across the conflict zone — the result of burning oil infrastructure producing chemical-laden particulate matter falling across populated areas of Iran, Iraq, and the Gulf. The warning extends to disrupted supply chains for life-saving humanitarian goods — medical equipment, food aid, emergency fuel — that cannot move through a region where insurance has collapsed and shipping routes are contested.

A Red Cross medic and a Catholic priest — both killed in separate Israeli strikes on Lebanon this week — were buried Wednesday. The ICRC has now lost personnel in the active conflict zone for the first time since 2006. Iran accused the US and Israel of deliberately targeting civilians, with strikes killing more than 1,300 Iranian civilians since the war began. The NYT reported earlier this week that preliminary findings indicate at least one US strike was conducted using outdated targeting data — characterised by UN human rights experts as a potential war crime under the Rome Statute.

Diplomacy · The Exit Ramp Takes Shape
Ceasefire Contacts · Beijing · Paris · Moscow
Three powers
contacted Iran.
None is America.

Iran's Deputy Foreign Minister confirmed Wednesday that China, Russia, and France have all contacted Tehran regarding a ceasefire framework. None of those contacts involves the United States directly. Trump said the war would end "very soon" — while Defence Secretary Hegseth said it would not end until "the enemy is totally and decisively defeated." Both statements were made on the same day. They are not compatible.

The diplomatic picture: the exit runs through Beijing. China has the economic leverage over Iran that no Western power holds, a confirmed shipping lane through Hormuz, and a strategic interest in a settlement that preserves its corridor. Every day China's envoy Zhai Jun moves through the region without committing is a day Beijing's leverage grows over all parties simultaneously. Russia's contact with Iran adds a second non-Western track. France's involvement is the European credibility layer a deal would need.

A ceasefire is forming in outline. But outline is not mechanism, and mechanism is not timeline. The Global South cannot afford to wait for the architecture to be built. The September food spike is already locked in regardless of when this ends.

Meridian · Day 11 Assessment · March 11, 2026
The market blinked.
The war didn't.

Today will be reported as the day oil "crashed." It did not crash. It fell from a historic spike to a level that is still catastrophic for every import-dependent economy in the Global South. The tweet that moved markets $20 in thirty minutes is the story of a war whose economic consequences are now running entirely on information asymmetry and fear — which is precisely the most dangerous environment for the poorest and least-informed participants in the global economy.

The Lebanon front is now fully open. The joint Iran-Hezbollah operation tonight is not an escalation of rhetoric — it is a structural change in how the war is being fought. Two fronts, two missile systems, coordinated targeting. Israel's threat to strike Lebanese civilian infrastructure is not a bluff — it is the logical next step in the IDF's escalation doctrine. What that means for Lebanon's already-shattered population of 800,000 displaced is a humanitarian emergency that has not yet reached its worst point.

Qatar's force majeure on LNG exports is the story the Caribbean and South Asia are not yet discussing loudly enough. Qatar supplies 20% of global LNG. South Asia's power grids — Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka — run on that supply. The IEA's reserve release covers oil, not gas. The LNG gap is unfilled, unaddressed, and accelerating the energy crisis in three of the world's most exposed economies. This is what Day 11 actually is for the Global South: not oil relief, but a compounding crisis with a new fuel type added to the emergency.

The ceasefire contacts are real. The exit ramp exists. But it is being built in Beijing, Paris, and Moscow — not Washington. And while it is being built, the cascade continues. The Global South needs intelligence, not reassurance. That is what Meridian is here for.
